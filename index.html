01 · The operating model triad

Practice, GCC/GBS and Center of Excellence

Three constructs enterprises reach for when a capability needs to scale — often used interchangeably, but each answers a different question.

How they work in tandem

Picture GCC/GBS as the factory, Practice as the assembly lines inside it, and the CoE as the R&D and quality-engineering function that improves every line, regardless of which factory it runs in.

Example

A global insurer's Claims practice supplied the adjusters; its GCC in Manila ran claims processing at scale for five functions; a central Claims Excellence CoE set the quality standard and fraud-detection playbook used by every claims team, wherever it sat.

03 · Setting up a CoE

Vision, stakeholders and deliverables

Start from a named business problem, not an org-chart ambition. Secure a sponsor and define scope boundaries before hiring.

Vision statement template

  • Who we serve — the practices, geographies or business units the CoE exists to uplift
  • What excellence looks like — the 2-3 outcomes that define success
  • How we get there — standards, accelerators, governance, enablement
  • By when — a credible horizon, usually 12-24 months to embedded maturity

04 · Resourcing the CoE

Models and trade-offs

Resourcing is where most CoEs fail in practice — the choice is rarely revisited as demand and maturity change.

Example

A Data & Analytics CoE started virtual — two senior analysts at 20% time building a data catalog. Within a year, demand outgrew part-time bandwidth, and it converted to a small fully centralized team of four, with catalog stewardship federated to data owners.

06 · Core activities, OKRs/KPIs and reporting

What the CoE does, and how it proves it

Every CoE runs the same six core activities. What differs is the KPI set that proves impact.

Example

An AI CoE reported "40 prompts published" and got a lukewarm response. The next quarter it reported "40 prompts published, 12 in active production use, saving an estimated 6 hours per week per adopting team" — same activity, a materially different conversation with leadership.
07 · Knowledge management

The CoE's real asset

A CoE's authority comes from being the trusted, current source of accumulated knowledge. Strip that away and it's just an org-chart box.

Explicit knowledge

Documented standards, templates, playbooks, policies — easy to capture, easy to publish, but only ever the surface layer.

Tacit knowledge

The judgment senior practitioners apply that never makes a template — why a risk was escalated, why a standard approach was deliberately skipped. Harder to capture, usually where the real value sits.

Common anti-patterns to avoid

The CXO value narrative

Translate CoE output into the three things a P&L owner tracks: cost avoided, risk reduced, speed gained. "We published 40 standards" won't survive a budget review. "Standardized delivery cut program overruns by X% and avoided Y in rework" will.

09 · People management

The CoE is only as good as who staffs it

Structurally sound CoEs quietly fail by treating headcount as a line item rather than a talent strategy.

Competency & career

  • A skills ladder tied to recognized levels — CoE tenure as a credible resume line
  • A rotation model: 6-12 months in, then back to delivery — keeps the CoE grounded
  • CoE contribution counted explicitly in performance review and promotion

Retention & succession

  • Protect core roles from being pulled back to billable delivery first
  • Build a two-deep succession bench for the CoE lead role
  • Offer a dual career ladder — technical depth and CoE leadership

11 · Building awareness

An internal marketing campaign for the CoE

A CoE nobody has heard of cannot be adopted, no matter how good its standards are. Run it through a funnel, not a one-time announcement.

Example

An AI CoE's launch email alone generated awareness but almost no usage. Adding a two-line "try this one prompt template in your next deck" call-to-action moved measurably more people into actual trial than three follow-up announcements combined.

12 · Scalability and adaptability

Designed to flex, not just to launch

The org, the demand, and the CoE's own scope will all change well before it reaches full maturity.

Governing multiple CoEs

Once an organization runs more than one CoE, a light "CoE of CoEs" layer — a quarterly cross-lead forum — prevents duplicated standards and competing asks on the same practitioners.

Resilience to org change

Document the operating model independently of any one leader. During M&A, treat the CoE's standards as a candidate baseline for the combined entity.

13 · Closing perspective

The human layer decides the outcome

A Center of Excellence earns its name only when judged against a standard: measurable capability uplift, adoption that survives without enforcement, and a value story a CXO would repeat in their own words. The difference between a CoE that becomes a durable capability and one that quietly disappears at the next reorg almost always comes down to the human layer — whether the people in it want to stay, whether the people around it actually change how they work, and whether the whole design can flex as the organization does.

Key principles

✓ Practice delivers, GCC scales, CoE elevates — know which one you need
✓ Standards without adoption are just documents
✓ Treat the CoE's comms as a real marketing campaign, not an announcement
✓ Knowledge management is the CoE's core asset, not a byproduct
✓ Fund, resource and govern the CoE with a lifecycle in mind — not just a launch
✓ Report outcomes, not activity
